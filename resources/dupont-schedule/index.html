The DuPont Schedule: How It Works, Pros and Cons

A 12-hour, four-crew rotation that covers 24/7 and gives every crew a full week off every four weeks.

Schedule Pattern
Home›Resources›Shift Schedule Patterns›DuPont Schedule
Schedule PatternsBy Jim Dillingham, Founder & Senior ConsultantOctober 20265 min read
In brief

The DuPont schedule is a 12-hour rotating schedule that uses four crews to cover a 24/7 operation. Over a 28-day cycle, each crew works four nights, three days, three nights and four days, with breaks between them — and then gets seven days off in a row. Crews average 42 hours a week, have two weekends off out of every four, and share day and night work evenly.

What Is the DuPont Schedule?

The DuPont schedule is one of the best-known 12-hour schedules for continuous operations. Four crews rotate through the same 28-day pattern, staggered by a week, so that two crews are always on shift (one on days, one on nights) while the other two are off. Its defining feature is the seven-day break that ends every cycle.

How Does the DuPont Schedule Work?

Each crew works the same sequence: four nights, three off, three days, one off, three nights, three off, four days, then seven days off. That is 14 twelve-hour shifts in 28 days. The grid below shows one crew’s four weeks, starting with the week off.

What Are the Advantages of the DuPont Schedule?

  • A full week off every month. The seven-day break is the main reason employees choose the DuPont. It allows real time away without using vacation.
  • Half the days of the year off. Like most 12-hour schedules, crews work about 182 days a year instead of about 260 on a typical 8-hour, five-day schedule.
  • Short work blocks. No one works more than four 12-hour shifts in a row.
  • Night work is shared. Every crew works the same number of nights, so no group is permanently on nights.

What Are the Drawbacks of the DuPont Schedule?

  • Frequent switches between days and nights. Each crew changes between day and night shifts four times every cycle, which some people find hard on sleep.
  • Weekends come in pairs. Crews get two weekends off in a row, then work two in a row.
  • Built-in overtime. At an average of 42 hours a week, some weeks run over 40 hours. In the U.S. those hours are paid at overtime rates, so the cost has to be planned for.
  • Long breaks complicate coverage. With a crew always on its week off, covering vacations and absences takes deliberate planning.

When Does the DuPont Schedule Fit?

The DuPont tends to fit 24/7 operations that want 12-hour shifts, want to share night work across everyone, and have a workforce that values long breaks over short, frequent ones. It is a weaker fit where most employees would rather work fixed days or fixed nights — in our surveys, about 85% of shift workers prefer fixed shifts to rotating ones — or where the operation can’t absorb crews being away for a full week.
