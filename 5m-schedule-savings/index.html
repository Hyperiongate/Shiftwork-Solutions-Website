The Background

When Incremental Decisions Become an Invisible Tax

The distribution center ran around the clock — roughly 2,000 employees across Replenishment, Processing, Maintenance, and support functions, on a mix of 8- and 12-hour shifts.

Like most large facilities, the schedule had grown by accretion: one scheduling decision layered on top of the last, year after year. No single manager had designed the whole thing or fully understood it.

Leadership came to us with what looked like three separate problems:

  • OvertimeRunning persistently above benchmark.
  • Supervisor timeSupervisors consumed by the complexity of shift handoffs.
  • Weekend turnoverStubborn turnover in the departments that carried weekend coverage.

The schedule was the common thread in all three. No one had yet looked at it as a single system.

Finding the Cost

Mapping the Schedule As It Actually Ran

Our business assessment mapped the schedule as it actually ran — every overlap, every start time, department by department — not as it was assumed to run. Two hidden costs came out of it.

Cost #1

Accidental Overlap

$3.8Mmidpoint / year

Across five departments, shifts were starting and stopping in ways that put people on the clock at the same time. They were doing handoff work that produced no additional output.

Measured range: $3.4M to $4.2M a year against the real schedule.
Cost #2

Coordination Friction

$1.6M/ year

Departments were operating with two to five different start times at once. The friction quietly drained productive time from roughly 1,250 employees, every day.

Why it stayed hidden: it was not a line item anywhere.
The Redesign

Fixing the Root Cause, Not the Symptoms

The work followed the four-phase methodology we use on every engagement: business assessment, workforce assessment, solution design, and implementation.

01

Consolidated Overlap

Reduced across the five departments to the minimum genuinely useful for clean handoffs — deliberate rather than accidental.

02

Standardized Start Times

One start time per shift in each department. Supervisors got a consistent reference point and less administrative friction.

03

Dedicated Weekend Crew

Weekend coverage rebuilt around a dedicated crew. Employees saw it as a better deal than five days with an unpredictable weekend day.

Quantified Results

Saving Money Without Sacrificing Your People

Both root causes were addressed in a single redesign, recovering more than $5 million in annual labor cost without reducing headcount or operating hours.

The Pattern Underneath

Why Good Operations Carry Invisible Schedule Costs

Overlap costs and start-time complexity are not fixed features of operating at scale. They are the accumulated result of scheduling decisions made one at a time — each sensible on its own day, never examined together as a system.

That is why the cost survives: it gets normalized into the budget, and no one ever adds it up. When someone finally does, the number is almost always larger than leadership expects.

And a good deal of it is recoverable, because it was never buying productivity in the first place.

The key insight for leadership

Most schedules are not designed from scratch — they accrete over time. Unearned overlap is labor cost you can remove without cutting capacity.
