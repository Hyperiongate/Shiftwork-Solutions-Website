From the Session
What we covered

Six threads, one argument: the technology is real, it is arriving faster in some places than others, and the operations that plan for it deliberately will be the ones that benefit from it.

One
How we got here

A short history of artificial intelligence and why it felt sudden. ChatGPT arrived in late 2022, but the groundwork had been laid for decades. Knowing the timeline makes the current pace easier to judge.

Two
What a large language model actually is

In plain language, without the math. What these systems are genuinely good at, where they fail, and why the difference matters when you decide what to trust one with.

Three
What an AI agent is

The difference between a system that answers a question and one that takes an action on your behalf. This is the shift that changes day-to-day work in an operation.

Four
AI and training

Training built around the individual learner rather than the class, demonstrated live. The same approach applies to onboarding a new operator on equipment your crews already run.

Five
Robotics and the pace of rollout

What is actually on plant floors today versus what is on a demo reel, and how quickly the gap between the two is closing.

Six
Supply chain and production optimization

Feeding a paper machine manual into a system that can then identify every part, tell you what to order, and flag when to order it — plus what this does to the economics of crewing a 24/7 operation.

The Core Argument

AI does not have to replace jobs. It makes people more efficient — the same way computers did.

The planner steps back to the bigger picture instead of grinding through the arithmetic. Maintenance gets planned better rather than eliminated. The operations that treat this as a staffing cut will get a staffing cut. The ones that treat it as leverage will get leverage.
